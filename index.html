How it plays

Build. Pave. Beat the bell.

Each week someone on campus asks for a building, and you decide where it goes. Then the bell rings every 45 seconds. Anyone still walking when it rings is late, and ten late in one period ends the run.

  1. One

    They cut the corner

    Wherever you put a building, students take the cheapest route to it. If your paving is too much of a detour, they ignore it and walk on the grass. Grass is slow, it wears into a trail, then into mud, and mud is slower.

  2. Two

    Pave where they walk

    The clock waits after you place a building. Drag to lay paving stones along the lines they will walk, then ring the bell. Drag along a path to widen it. Press and hold, then drag, to lift paving nobody uses: the stones come back in full.

  3. Three

    Beat the bell

    Get everyone to class before it rings, and each bell pays you stones for the students who made it. When a run ends, the student paper prints the headline, and a replay shows where they were held up and says why.

What is in the game

A crowd you persuade, not command.

You never tell a student where to go. You make the right way the easy way.

Students with their own ideas

Every student takes the cheapest route. Paving only works if it is close to the way they wanted to go anyway.

Grass, trail, mud

Feet wear the grass into a trail, then into mud, and mud slows everyone who crosses it.

A toolbox from real practice

Paving, a second lane, stepping stones that save the grass, footbridges, steps cut into a slope, hedges, keep-off-the-grass signs and a gate through the wall. Each one is something people who manage real paths do, and a field guide in the game says where it comes from.

See the next rush

Before the next bell, faint lines show where students will walk. While you drag, they snap onto your path only if students would actually use it.

The problem, while you can fix it

Students who will not make the bell wear an amber ring, and a pin marks where they are losing time. When a run ends, a replay shows where they were held up and one sentence tells you the reason.

You build the campus

Every week three people ask for something: a library, a science lab, a dining hall, a bus stop. Take one and place it anywhere on the lawn, with the walks it will create drawn before you commit. Each gives something in thanks: stones, a footbridge, a hedge or signs.

The Dean, the paper and a career

The Dean sets three goals a term: enrol enough students, keep lateness down, keep the lawn. Meet all three for an A. The student paper prints your result as a front page, and a good grade earns an invitation to the next campus.

Felt and heard

A haptic tick on every stone, and sound that is generated in the app as you play. No licensed music.

Four campuses

Each campus has a landmark and one idea.

The campuses are invented places. Each one bends the shortest line a different way. You start at Alder Quad and are invited to the next as your grades allow.

  • Landmark: clock tower

    Alder Quad

    A walled quad around a clock tower. The walls leave only so many ways through.

  • Landmark: old mill

    Millbrook

    A stream runs through it, with a single ford. Footbridges are the prize.

  • Landmark: boathouse

    Lakeshore

    A lake lies right across the shortest line. Somebody has to go round.

  • Landmark: observatory

    Castle Hill

    A slope nobody wants to climb. The straight path is not always the one they take.

Four ways to play

Chase a grade, or just tend the lawn.

  • Term

    Four weeks of building and an exam week, then the Dean's report and a grade from A to F.

  • Endless

    Keep the campus running for as long as you can keep them on time.

  • Daily Campus

    One seeded campus a day, the same for everyone, with a Game Center leaderboard and a card to share.

  • Garden

    No bell, no failure and unlimited stones.
