Support

Help with Desire Lines: how the game works, and how to reach us.

Get in touch

Email support@denvered.com. It helps to say which device you have, your iOS version, the campus and mode you were playing, and what you expected to happen. A screenshot is welcome.

The bell

How does the bell work?

The bell rings every 45 seconds. Between bells, students walk from one class to the next. Anyone still walking when the bell rings is late.

How many late students end a run?

Ten late in one period ends the run. A late student turns red and gets a ring, so you can see where on the campus they were held up.

What happens when a run ends?

A replay shows where students were held up, and one sentence says why. It is the quickest way to see what to change next time.

What are periods, weeks and terms?

A week is four periods: Arrive, Change, Lunch and Afternoon. A term is four weeks, each starting with a request, and then an exam week, held in your largest teaching building. After that comes the Dean's report and a grade from A to F.

Paths and students

Why are students ignoring my path?

Students take the cheapest route. If your paving is too much of a detour compared with cutting across the lawn, they ignore it and walk on the grass.

Before the next bell, faint lines show where students will walk next. While you drag a path, those lines snap onto it only if students would actually use it. If the lines stay on the grass, that path will be ignored: try a line closer to the one they want.

Why does the grass matter?

Grass wears into a trail, then into mud, and mud is slow. A muddy shortcut can make students late even though it is the shortest way.

How do I lay paving?

Drag across the lawn to lay paving stones. Your supply is limited, and the count is shown at the bottom of the screen. Each bell pays a few more for the students who were on time. A trail is a good guide: it is the path students already want.

How do I widen a path?

Drag along a path you have already laid to widen it. It helps where a crowd is too big for one path.

How do I lift paving I no longer want?

Press and hold on the paving, then drag along it to lift it. The stones come back in full, so trying a path costs you nothing.

What are requests?

Each week three people on campus ask for something: a teaching building, a halls block, a dining hall, a bus stop or a car park. Take one and it appears on the lawn. Drag it where you want it: faint lines show the walks it will create, and it turns red where it cannot go. Place commits it, and it cannot be moved afterwards. Each request gives something in thanks: stones, a footbridge, a hedge or signs. You can also pass and take a few stones.

Why is the clock stopped after I place a building?

So you can get ready for it. The lines of the coming bell are drawn on the lawn, and you can pave them before anyone sets off. Tap Ring the bell when you are ready and the week begins.

What are the other tools for?

The disc beside your stones opens the toolbox for the campus you are on. Stepping stones make a path that still counts as lawn, in single file. A footbridge crosses up to four cells of water. Steps take the climb out of a slope. A hedge closes a short cut. A sign keeps unhurried students off the grass near it; anyone in a hurry ignores it. A gate opens a wall for good. Everything but a gate comes back in full when you lift it.

What does the Dean want?

Three things each term: enrol a number of students, keep the term's late count under a number, and keep a share of the lawn. The grade is how many you met: three is an A, two a B, one a C, none a D, and a failed bell is an F. The goals are on the pause screen.

How do I unlock the other campuses?

You start at Alder Quad. Finishing a term there with a C or better brings an invitation to Millbrook, then Lakeshore, then Castle Hill. Daily Campus and Garden are open from the start.

What are the ways to play?

Term plays a term for a grade. Endless keeps going. Daily Campus is one seeded campus a day, the same for everyone, with a Game Center leaderboard and a card to share. Garden has no bell, no failure and unlimited stones.

Game Center, progress and devices

Do I need to sign in to Game Center?

No. Game Center is optional. It is used for leaderboards, including the Daily Campus leaderboard, and for achievements.

To sign in or out, open the Settings app on your device and choose Game Center. Game Center is Apple's service, and Apple handles the information it uses under Apple's privacy policy.

How do I reset my progress?

Open Settings in the game and choose to reset your progress. Your progress is stored only on your device and we hold no copy, so a reset cannot be undone. Scores already posted to Game Center are held by Apple and are not removed by a reset.

Does it run on iPad and Mac?

Yes. Desire Lines is made for iPhone and iPad, in portrait, and requires iOS 18 or later. It also runs on Apple silicon Macs and on Apple Vision Pro as the iPad app.

Is there an account?

No. Desire Lines has no accounts of its own, and your progress is saved on your device.

Purchase and privacy

What does it cost?

$4.99, paid once on the App Store. There are no in-app purchases and no ads.

How do I get a refund?

Apple handles all payments and refunds. Ask for one at reportaproblem.apple.com.

What data does the game collect?

None. Its privacy label is Data Not Collected. The privacy policy has the detail.

I am testing a beta. What is the diagnostics file?

TestFlight builds keep a diagnostics file on your device. It is never transmitted. If you want to help with a problem, you can export it and share it with us yourself. Builds from the App Store do not write this file.
